# Objection Handler

Scripted, real-world responses to every major real estate objection — organized by who you're talking to and what they're pushing back on. The best response to an objection is never defensive. It's curious, empathetic, and redirecting toward the outcome that actually serves the client.

## Framework — Before Every Objection Response

Use this 3-step structure internally before responding:

1. **Acknowledge** — Show you heard them, without agreeing that the objection should stop the conversation
2. **Clarify** — Uncover whether this is a real objection or a surface concern
3. **Respond** — Answer the actual concern, not the surface statement

> "That's totally understandable — can I ask what's driving that specifically? I want to make sure I address the right thing."

---

## SECTION 1 — Agent vs. Seller (Listing)

### "Your commission is too high / I want to pay less."

> "I completely understand — it's a real number and I don't take it lightly. Can I share how I think about it? Most sellers who focus on the commission end up focused on the wrong number. What actually matters is your net proceeds — what you walk away with after everything. An agent who negotiates full commission on your behalf is usually the same agent who negotiates full price for your home. An agent who folds on their own fee is the same agent who'll fold when a buyer pushes back on your price. I'd rather show you what I'll get you net and let that be the conversation."

---

### "Another agent said they'd list it for [lower commission]."

> "That's worth understanding — can I ask, did they walk you through how they plan to achieve that? Lower commission sometimes means fewer resources going into the marketing, photography, or agent cooperation. It's also worth asking: if they're willing to reduce their fee quickly to get the listing, how will they perform when it comes time to negotiate your sale price? I'm not trying to talk you out of a conversation — I just want you to have the full picture before you decide."

---

### "We want to try [higher price] first and see what happens."

> "I hear you — and I want to honor your equity. Here's what the data tells us: homes that launch at the right price attract the most buyers in the first 2 weeks, which is when your leverage is highest. When a home sits and reduces, buyers wonder what's wrong with it — even when nothing is. What I'd propose is this: let's go to market at [recommended price], and if we don't have an offer in [X days], we revisit the number together. You're not locked in forever. You're just starting smart."

---

### "We already talked to [other agent] / we're thinking of going with them."

> "I appreciate you telling me that — I'd rather know than not. Can I ask what it is about their proposal that's appealing? I'm asking because I want to understand if it's something I can address, or if it's a genuinely better fit. Either way, I want you to make the best decision for your situation — I'd just like the chance to make sure you have the full picture."

---

### "I want to sell it myself (FSBO)."

> "That makes complete sense — you know the home better than anyone, and the commission savings are real. I have a question: what's your plan for pricing, marketing exposure, and handling the legal documents? Most FSBO sellers are very capable of showing the home — where it gets complicated is the negotiation, the inspection process, and the contract. I've helped sellers navigate all of those. If you're open to it, I'd love to just walk you through what a traditional sale looks like in your area and let you compare. No pressure to use me — just want you informed."

---

### "I'm not ready to sell yet / we're thinking about it for next year."

> "That's perfect timing to talk, honestly. The worst time to plan a sale is the week you decide to list. What I can do for you right now is pull together a current market analysis so you have a real number to plan around, and we can talk through what you'd want to do to prepare. No commitment required. Does that sound useful?"

---

## SECTION 2 — Agent vs. Buyer

### "I want to wait for the market to go down."

> "That's a strategy — and it might be right for your situation. Can I ask what you think 'the market going down' looks like? Because what typically happens when prices drop is that interest rates are higher, which can actually cost you more per month. And in [Market], we're not seeing a significant correction coming — inventory is still [low/limited/steady]. The question I'd ask is: if the market stays flat for the next [12–18] months and rates stay where they are, will you wish you'd acted? I can't predict the market — but I can help you understand what the data actually suggests."

---

### "Interest rates are too high right now."

> "The rates are real and they matter — I won't dismiss that. But here's something most buyers don't realize: you're buying the house, and you can refinance the rate. The buyers who wait for rates to drop are usually buying in a more competitive market at higher prices because everyone waited for the same thing. Locking in your home at today's price and refinancing when rates move can end up being the smarter financial play. I can connect you with a lender who can model this out for your specific situation — would that be helpful?"

---

### "We're already working with another agent."

> "I respect that — I wouldn't want someone going around me either. Can I ask how that's going for you? Are you finding properties, getting your questions answered? I ask because sometimes buyers are 'working with an agent' in a loose sense and not in a committed relationship. If you're happy, that's great. If there are gaps, I want to know. Either way, I appreciate you being upfront with me."

---

### "We want to think about it / we're not ready to make an offer."

> "Of course — what would help you get there? I ask because sometimes 'thinking about it' means there's a specific concern that hasn't been addressed yet, and I'd rather know what that is than leave you with it unanswered. Is it the price? The neighborhood? Something about the home itself? Let's talk through it — even if the answer is it's just not the right one, that's useful too."

---

### "We want to offer [significantly below list price]."

> "I can absolutely write that offer for you — it's your decision, and I'm here to represent you. What I want to do first is walk you through the comps so you can make an informed choice. In this market, [homes at this price point are getting X% of list / this seller has been sitting X days and may be negotiable / I don't think this will get accepted and here's why]. I want you to go in with the best chance of success — whether that's this offer or a revised one."

---

## SECTION 3 — Investor / Wholesaler vs. Motivated Seller

### "Your offer is too low / I need more."

> "I hear you — and I want to be fair with you. My offer is based on [the cost to repair the property / what buyers are paying for homes in this condition / what I need to make the numbers work for me]. Can I ask what number you had in mind? If there's a gap, I want to understand it and see if there's a way to bridge it — maybe through terms, timeline, or something else. Tell me what you're working with."

---

### "I'm going to try listing it first."

> "That makes sense — you might get a higher price on the open market. A few things to think through: listing means showings, inspections, potentially buyer financing falling through, and closing timelines of 30–60 days minimum. My offer is [cash / fast close / as-is] — no contingencies. If listing works great for you, I understand. But if it doesn't, I'm still here. Can I stay in touch?"

---

### "I don't want to sell / I just need time."

> "I completely respect that — and there's no pressure here. Can I ask what would need to change for selling to feel like the right move? Sometimes people aren't ready to sell today, but they have a number in mind or a situation that would make it make sense. I just want to understand your world a little better so I can be helpful when the timing is right."

---

### "I had another investor offer me more."

> "That's great — and you should take the best deal available to you. Before you close that door, can I ask a few questions? Was that offer in writing? Was it cash or contingent on financing? Did they give you a specific closing date? Sometimes the highest verbal offer isn't the strongest offer when you look at the full picture. I want you to make the best decision — if theirs is truly better, you should take it."

---

## SECTION 4 — Brokerage Owner vs. Agent (Recruiting)

### "I'm happy where I am."

> "That's actually the best thing I can hear — I'm not looking for frustrated agents, I'm looking for good ones. What would need to be different for you to even consider a conversation? If nothing would make you consider it, I respect that. But if there's one thing you'd change about your current situation, I'd love to know what it is."

---

### "I don't want to lose my clients / my book of business."

> "Your book of business is yours — it goes where you go. Your clients chose you, not your brokerage. The only risk to your relationships is how you handle the transition, and I've helped agents do this smoothly dozens of times. There's a right way to communicate it to your clients that actually strengthens the relationship rather than disrupting it. Would it help to walk through what that looks like?"

---

### "Your splits aren't as good as [competitor]."

> "Let's put the actual numbers side by side — I'd rather show you than tell you. A lot of agents are comparing percentages without accounting for the cap, the fees, the desk costs, and what they're getting for the money. When we add it all up, the picture often looks different. Can I walk you through what an agent at your production level would actually net with us vs. where you are now?"

---

### "I just renewed my agreement / I'm locked in."

> "Most agreements aren't as binding as agents think — real estate independent contractor agreements are typically at-will in most states. You might want to have an attorney look at what you signed. And even if there's a notice period, that just means we plan your transition a little further out. It's not usually a barrier — just a timeline."

## Quick Reference — Objection by Situation

| Persona | Objection | Core Response Angle |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| Seller | Commission too high | Net proceeds > commission; fold on fee = fold in negotiation |
| Seller | Want to test higher price | First 2 weeks are gold; price reductions hurt more than they help |
| Seller | Going FSBO | Understand their plan; surface the gaps; offer value without pressure |
| Buyer | Wait for market to drop | Rates + price together; timing the market vs. time in the market |
| Buyer | Rates too high | Buy the house, refinance the rate |
| Investor | Offer too low | Clarify their number; bridge with terms/timeline |
| Investor | Another investor offered more | Written offer? Cash? Specific close date? Full picture |
| Agent recruit | Happy where I am | What would change it? Lead with curiosity |
| Agent recruit | Losing clients | Book of business is yours; how to transition correctly |
