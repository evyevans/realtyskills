# Objection Handling Coach — detailed procedure

## Understand the concern

Quote or accurately paraphrase the actual objection and identify the question it raises: price evidence, affordability, service value, uncertainty, relationship trust, representation, timing, or another issue. Treat emotion as the user's reported observation, not a psychological diagnosis. A firm refusal or opt-out ends persuasion; do not relabel it a “stall.”

Ask one useful discovery question that invites the client to explain their criteria. Separate facts the agent can establish from predictions nobody can promise. Where compensation is discussed, use actual negotiable terms and disclose the scope of service; do not invoke a standard commission or imply another agent cannot deliver value because of their fee.

## Response framework

Acknowledge the stated concern; ask or answer the relevant question; provide sourced context or a transparent comparison; explain realistic options and tradeoffs; and invite a voluntary next step. Provide a concise spoken response, a consultative alternative, and a written follow-up when relevant. Use the user's tone and requested channel. Do not fabricate buyer demand, future rates, appreciation, offers, experience, past results, or deadline pressure.

For price objections, compare the actual evidence and alternatives, not unsupported promises. For fee objections, explain services and user-approved options without guaranteed savings. For market-timing concerns, distinguish current facts from scenarios. For financing or legal issues, prepare specific lender or counsel questions. For representation conflicts, respect existing agreements and avoid inducing breach.

## Practice mode

If requested, play the client one turn at a time using only the provided scenario, then evaluate the agent's reply for listening, factual accuracy, clarity, and respect for choice. Do not invent private motives or pretend the role-play predicts an actual outcome. For asynchronous use, provide likely follow-up questions and draft branches rather than requiring live practice before delivering the scripts.

## Deliverable

Return concern summary and unknowns; discovery questions; finished spoken/written responses; branches for acceptance, further concern, delay, and refusal; optional practice exercise; and a concise coaching rubric. Include evidence needed to support any quantitative claim and a concrete, consent-based next step.

## Before delivery

Responses answer the actual concern, acknowledge legitimate tradeoffs, use no pressure or fabricated success claims, and stop after a refusal. Coaching identifies specific improvements.
