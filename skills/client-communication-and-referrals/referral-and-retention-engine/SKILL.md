# Referral & Retention Engine

The most sustainable real estate business runs on referrals. Not leads. Not ads. Referrals. Clients who trust you refer people without being asked — but clients who are asked the right way, at the right time, refer people consistently.

This skill powers the post-close relationship: the messages, the scripts, the sequences, and the touchpoints that keep you in your clients' minds long after closing day.

## When to Use

- Setting up post-close follow-up for a new client
- Crafting a referral ask (soft or direct)
- Requesting a Google review or testimonial
- Re-engaging a database that's gone cold
- Building an annual calendar of client touchpoints
- Preparing a specific anniversary or check-in message

## SEQUENCE 1 — Post-Close Follow-Up (Buyer)

The 12-month relationship timeline after a buyer closes.

| Touch | Timing | Channel | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|---------|
| 1 | Day 3 post-close | Text | Warm check-in |
| 2 | Day 10 | Email | Practical support |
| 3 | Day 30 | Text or Call | 1-month check-in |
| 4 | Day 90 | Email | Equity/market update |
| 5 | Day 180 (6 mo.) | Email + Review request | Review ask |
| 6 | Month 11 | Email | Annual anniversary setup |
| 7 | Month 12 (1 yr.) | Card + Text | Home anniversary |

---

**Touch 1 — Day 3 Text:**
> Hey [Name]! Hope the move is going well 🏡 If you need contractor referrals, a handyman, or literally anything — I'm here. Let me know how everything's feeling in the new place!

**Touch 2 — Day 10 Email:**
> Subject: Settling in? A few things worth knowing
>
> Hi [Name],
>

## REFERRAL ASKS

### Soft Referral Ask (Use First)

Best in the first 6–12 months when relationship is fresh.

**Email:**
> Subject: A quick thought
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I've been thinking about you and wanted to reach out. I love what I do, and the best part of this work is the people I get to help.
>
> I don't advertise — I build my business on referrals from clients I've genuinely taken care of. So if you know anyone who's thinking about buying or selling — a coworker, a friend, a family member — I'd be honored if you thought of me. A simple introduction is all it takes.
>
> No pressure at all. Just wanted you to know that I appreciate your trust, and I'm always here for anyone in your circle.
>
> [Agent Name] | [Phone]

**Text Version (Soft):**
> Hey [Name]! Quick thought — if you ever hear of anyone looking to buy or sell in [Area], I'd love if you kept me in mind. Word of mouth is everything to my business. Thanks so much 🙏

---

### Direct Referral Ask (Use After Establishing Value)

**Script for a Phone Call:**
> "Hey [Name], I'm so glad things are going well. I wanted to ask you something directly. I'm growing my business this year and I'm doing it through referrals from clients I've actually helped — people like you. Is there anyone in your life — a coworker, a neighbor, a family member — who's talked about buying or selling? Even if they're just thinking about it, a quick introduction from you carries more weight than anything I could do on my own. Would you be comfortable doing that?"

> *If they say yes:* "Who's the first person that comes to mind? I'll reach out and just introduce myself — no hard sell, I promise."

> *If they say they'll think about it:* "That's totally fine. If someone comes to mind, just shoot me a text with their name and number and I'll take it from there. Thank you."

---

### Referral Introduction Request (Asking Client to Make the Intro)

> "One more thing — if you know someone, would you be comfortable texting them something like: 'Hey, I worked with [Agent Name] to [buy/sell] my home and she was incredible. You should reach out.' That kind of intro is gold. Can I send you a draft text you could forward? I'll make it easy."

**Draft text you send the client to forward:**
> "Hey! I wanted to introduce you to my realtor, [Agent Name]. She helped me [buy/sell] and she was genuinely the best — super knowledgeable, communicative, and fought hard for me. If you're thinking about real estate at all, reach out. Her number is [Phone]. Tell her [Client Name] sent you!"

## RE-ENGAGEMENT — Cold Database (6+ Months Inactive)

### Email Re-Entry:

> Subject: It's been a while — checking in
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I realized it's been [months / a while] since we've connected and I wanted to remedy that.
>
> No pitch — just a genuine check-in. How are things going? Are you still in [City], or has life taken you somewhere new?
>
> If you're still in [Neighborhood / Area], I'd love to send you a quick market update on what's happening near your home. And if you've moved or your plans have changed, I'd love to know.
>
> Either way — I'm here whenever you need anything, and I never want people I've helped to feel like they disappeared once the transaction was done.
>
> [Agent Name] | [Phone]

### Text Re-Entry:
> Hey [Name], it's [Agent] — it's been too long! How are you? Hope everything's going well. Still in [Area]? 😊

## Testimonial / Review Request Templates

### After a strong experience — ask immediately:

**Text (same week as close):**
> [Name] — working with you was such a pleasure. If you felt the same way, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It helps families like yours find someone they can trust. Here's the link: [URL]. Takes 2 min — thank you! ❤️

### Video Testimonial Ask:

> "Hey [Name], quick ask — would you be open to recording a 30-second video on your phone just sharing what the experience was like working with me? It doesn't have to be perfect — just genuine. Those videos are worth more than any ad I could run. I can even send you a simple prompt to make it easy."

**Prompt to send them:**
> *"Just answer these 3 questions in your own words: What were you worried about before we started? What surprised you most about the process? And what would you tell a friend who was thinking about using [Agent Name]?"*
