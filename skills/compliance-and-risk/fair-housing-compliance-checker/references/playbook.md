# Housing Marketing Risk Review — detailed procedure

## Establish applicability

Confirm country, regional/local jurisdiction, sale/rental context, advertising medium, and whether targeting or screening criteria are part of the request. Collect full text and relevant visuals or campaign settings; text alone cannot prove logo presence or targeting behavior. Verify applicable current housing/civil-rights law and guidance with the responsible authority. Local protections and lawful exemptions differ; do not transplant U.S. categories or exceptions globally.

## Contextual passes

Review explicit preferences, exclusions, limitations, or representations about who should live in the property. Check whether seemingly indirect language or targeting may steer or discourage people through protected characteristics or proxies. Review demographic neighborhood descriptions, language restrictions, stereotyped “ideal occupants,” accessibility claims, assistance-animal handling where relevant, and application conditions. Mention of an accessibility feature or a protected-group term is not automatically unlawful; explain context and source rather than issuing keyword-based verdicts.

Check occupancy limits against the applicable legal, building, and factual basis; no universal persons-per-bedroom rule should be inserted. If a user claims a housing exemption, collect the specific legal basis and supporting facts for professional review rather than approve the exemption from a phrase like “adult community.”

Separate objective property/location facts from subjective claims about safety, schools, or community composition. Do not reproduce demographic steering in a supposedly neutral rewrite. Verify factual transit distance, facility access, or measured accessibility only when included in the marketing claim.

## Findings and rewrite

For each passage/settings issue, provide location, exact excerpt or setting, concern, applicable source or unresolved legal question, confidence, severity based on consequence, and a suggested rewrite/action. Use “potential concern” unless the evidence and verified authority support a specific conclusion. Distinguish law from guidance, brokerage policy, and platform terms.

Rewrite every requested item in full, preserving legitimate property information and required disclosures while removing unsupported preferences. Do not certify that the final copy is compliant, and do not assume unseen visuals or settings were reviewed.

## Deliverable

Return scope and limits; jurisdiction/source table; prioritized passage/settings findings; original-to-proposed edits; full revised content; facts still requiring verification; and a broker/counsel/platform review checklist. If no concern is identified, say “no concerns identified in the material reviewed” and explain unreviewed elements rather than promise legal safety.

## Before delivery

Findings have contextual reasons and applicable authority; no blanket banned-word list, automatic occupancy limit, unsupported exemption, or compliance guarantee. The full rewrite retains verified property information.

## Official starting points

[HUD rights and obligations](https://www.hud.gov/stat/fheo/rights-obligations) and [HUD housing advertising guidance](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/FHEO/documents/BBE%20Part%20109%20Fair%20Housing%20Advertising.pdf) for U.S. matters. Confirm current applicability and additional local protections.

These are jurisdiction-specific starting points, not universal rules or proof of current applicability. Open and verify the relevant source during use; choose the corresponding local authority elsewhere.
