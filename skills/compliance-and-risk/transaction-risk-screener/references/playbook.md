# Transaction Risk Review — detailed procedure

## Establish facts

Separate document absence, inconsistency, verified adverse evidence, and ordinary transaction variations. Confirm the local settlement process; a missing U.S.-style title company is not inherently suspicious elsewhere. Do not flag age, nationality, disability, remote communication needs, a new entity, or absence from the property as proof of fraud. Do not demand unrelated sensitive identifiers.

## Identity, authority, and title

Compare party identifiers across executed documents, authorized title records, official entity registries, and signatory authority documents. Note aliases/spelling/entity-name differences for reconciliation rather than assuming impersonation. For a power of attorney, trustee, estate, or entity sale, identify the precise authority document and professional confirmation needed. Do not certify title from a tax-assessment page or entity good-standing status alone.

Review recorded ownership and encumbrance evidence, conflicting transfers, unreconciled payoffs, consent requirements, and unusual document changes. A discrepancy warrants a verification question grounded in the record, not an allegation. Keep personal documents in secure authorized channels; do not paste full identification numbers into a general chat report.

## Payments and communications

Treat changed wire or payment instructions, unexplained recipient changes, spoofed domains, and pressure to bypass established settlement controls as concrete verification concerns. Recommend independently contacting the known settlement professional using a previously verified channel, not phone numbers or links in the suspicious message. Never validate bank instructions from email alone, redirect funds, or disclose credentials. If funds may already have been sent, prioritize the user's bank/settlement escalation procedure and local reporting channels; do not claim recovery is guaranteed.

## Current reporting obligations

Identify which official financial-crime, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, or settlement reporting requirements might actually apply to the jurisdiction, parties, reporting person, transfer, and current legal status. Check amendments, court orders, suspensions, exceptions, and effective dates. Do not reuse fixed geographic thresholds or assert that all entities must submit beneficial-ownership reports. The responsible compliance/title/legal professional determines applicability; the agent prepares evidence and questions, not an unrequested report submission.

## Deliverable

Provide document/verification status; prioritized concern table with source, consequence, confidence, owner, and remedy; payment-verification checklist; title/identity/authority questions; current-rule verification notes; unresolved holds; and next actions. Overall language should describe remaining verification risk rather than declare a person fraudulent or a transaction safe. Do not run private background checks or contact counterparties unless specifically authorized.

## Before delivery

No demographic profiling, unsupported fraud accusations, guessed payment verification, or stale reporting requirement. Each hold explains the evidence and the specific professional verification needed.

## Official starting points

[FinCEN residential real estate rule and current status](https://www.fincen.gov/rre) and [official FAQs](https://www.fincen.gov/rre-faqs). Check current legal status and scope at use; these are U.S.-specific sources.

These are jurisdiction-specific starting points, not universal rules or proof of current applicability. Open and verify the relevant source during use; choose the corresponding local authority elsewhere.
