# Maintenance Work Order — detailed procedure

## Triage before administration

Review reported fire, gas odor, active flooding, electrical hazards, loss of essential services, security, or other immediate danger first. Advise contacting the appropriate local emergency/utility service and following its safety instructions; do not assume an emergency phone number applies worldwide. Do not suggest hazardous troubleshooting or wait for a routine owner response before presenting immediate safety guidance. The agent has not dispatched help unless an authorized tool confirms it.

Distinguish reported symptoms from diagnosis. Do not infer water-heater faults, mold confirmation, structural safety, or tenant responsibility from a description. Ask for safe, necessary observations, not invasive tests. Priority depends on actual conditions, affected occupants' expressed access needs, local habitability duties, and the management procedure. Do not assign universal temperatures or response periods.

## Work order and authorization

Create one item for each issue, retaining the original report and exact known request time. Include reference, property/unit alias, symptom, potential safety concern, proposed priority and reason, trade needed, scope for assessment, status, assigned owner/vendor if confirmed, access/notice requirements, and evidence requested. Unknown diagnosis, time, vendor, and cost remain pending.

Use the management agreement and user instructions for spending/dispatch authority. Never default to an owner authorization amount. Separate emergency procedure, diagnostic authorization, quote approval, and repair/replacement approval. If the threshold or exception is unknown, identify the responsible person to confirm it while providing the work-order drafts. Cost ranges need current local quotes or cited estimates; otherwise say “quote required.”

Record only necessary access information in appropriate private channels. Do not put door codes, key locations, or tenant contact details in a public/shareable report. Verify local entry notice, consent, vendor licensing, insurance, and scheduling rules when applicable; do not presume a right of entry from a repair request.

## Communications and closure

Prepare tenant acknowledgment describing known receipt, next assessment step, safety routing if relevant, and how updates will be provided. Do not promise a vendor arrival or repair completion without confirmation. Write vendor assessment/scope instructions and owner quote/approval request with actual costs and limits. Avoid accusing the tenant or making legal liability statements.

Track acknowledgment, assignment, appointment confirmed, assessment, quote/approval, work in progress, inspection/testing, invoice reconciliation, tenant confirmation where appropriate, and closed/reopened status. Closure requires completion evidence; scheduling alone is not completion.

## Deliverable

Return safety/triage note; issue table; work-order drafts; vendor scope; tenant acknowledgment; owner approval request if needed; scheduling/access requirements; status tracker; and closure/invoice checklist. Clearly separate proposed dispatch from an authorized completed action.

## Before delivery

Every issue is included, reported symptoms are not diagnosed as facts, emergency routing precedes routine scheduling, no invented cost or authority exists, and closure requires evidence.
