# Seller Outreach Scripts

Channel-ready outreach scripts for every motivated seller situation — SMS, email, voicemail, and direct mail. Each is crafted for the specific psychology of that seller type. Generic scripts get ignored. Situation-specific scripts open conversations.

## When to Use

- Investor or wholesaler has a lead list to work
- Agent targeting FSBOs or expired listings
- Outreach to absentee owners, vacant properties, probate leads
- Direct mail campaigns to distressed lists
- Building a follow-up sequence for non-responders

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Seller Situation

Ask the user:
1. **What type of seller/lead list are you working?** (see situations below)
2. **What channels do you need?** (SMS, email, voicemail, direct mail — pick all that apply)
3. **What's your offer type?** (cash offer, creative financing, listing, lease option)
4. **Your name and company/brokerage name for signature lines**

## Seller Situation Library

### SITUATION 1 — FSBO (For Sale By Owner)

**Psychology:** Seller thinks they'll save the commission. They've decided they don't need an agent. Approach with respect — never lead with "you need an agent." Lead with value and information.

**SMS (under 160 characters):**
> Hi [Name], I saw your home at [Address] is for sale by owner. I work with cash buyers actively looking in [City]. Would you be open to a quick conversation? — [Your Name]

**Email:**
> Subject: Your home at [Address] — quick question
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed your home at [Address] is listed for sale. I'm [Name] with [Company], and I work with a network of pre-qualified buyers actively looking in [Neighborhood].
>
> I'm not here to list your home — I just want to understand your situation and see if there's a buyer in my network who might be a fit. No pressure, no obligation.
>
> Would you be open to a 5-minute call this week?
>
> [Name] | [Phone] | [Email]

**Voicemail (under 30 seconds):**
> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. I came across your home at [Address] and I work with cash buyers in [City] — I just wanted to reach out and see if you'd be open to a quick conversation. No pressure at all. My number is [Phone]. Have a great day."

**Direct Mail:**
> [Name],
>
> I noticed your home at [Address] is available.
>
> I'm a local investor/buyer's agent who works with buyers actively looking in [Neighborhood]. If you're open to it, I'd love to learn about your timeline and situation — sometimes we can put together something that works better than a traditional sale.
>
> Give me a call or text at [Phone]. No obligation.
>
> [Name] | [Company]

### SITUATION 2 — Expired Listing (Agent Outreach)

**Psychology:** Seller is frustrated. Their home sat on the market and didn't sell. They may blame the agent, the price, or the market. Lead with empathy and a different approach — not just "hire me instead."

**SMS:**
> Hi [Name], I saw your listing at [Address] came off the market recently. I have some thoughts on what could work differently. Would you be open to a quick call? — [Agent Name], [Brokerage]

**Email:**
> Subject: [Address] — what I'd do differently
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed your home at [Address] recently expired on the market. I know that's a frustrating experience, and I don't want to pile on with another generic pitch.
>
> I've looked at the listing and I have specific thoughts on what I believe held it back — and what I'd do differently. It's not always just about price.
>
> Would you be open to a 15-minute call? I'll give you my honest assessment, and if it doesn't make sense to work together, no hard feelings.
>
> [Agent Name] | [Brokerage] | [Phone]

**Voicemail:**
> "Hi [Name], this is [Agent Name] with [Brokerage]. I saw your home at [Address] was recently on the market and I wanted to reach out. I have some specific thoughts on what could work differently — it's not a generic pitch. My number is [Phone]. I'd love 15 minutes with you. Thanks."

### SITUATION 3 — Pre-Foreclosure / Notice of Default

**Psychology:** Seller is in financial stress. They may feel shame, confusion, or urgency. Lead with empathy first. Never use the word "foreclosure" in first contact — use "situation with your mortgage" or "property at [Address]."

**SMS:**
> Hi [Name], I understand you may have a situation with your property at [Address]. I help homeowners in tough spots find solutions — sometimes selling quickly is the best path. I'm happy to talk confidentially. — [Name], [Phone]

**Email:**
> Subject: Options for [Address] — confidential
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm reaching out because I understand you may be navigating a difficult situation with your property at [Address]. I work with homeowners in similar circumstances every month, and I want you to know there are more options than most people realize.
>
> A fast, private sale can sometimes eliminate the stress entirely — and put cash in your hands before the process goes further.
>
> I'm happy to have a completely confidential conversation with no pressure. Would you be open to a call this week?
>
> [Name] | [Company] | [Phone]

**Direct Mail:**
> [Name],
>
> I work with homeowners who are facing difficult situations with their properties. If you're looking for a private, fast solution for [Address], I may be able to help.
>
> I buy homes directly — no agents, no fees, no lengthy process. If the timing is right for you, I'd love to talk.
>
> Call or text me anytime: [Phone]
>
> [Name] | [Company]
> *This is not a solicitation from a mortgage or legal company.*

### SITUATION 4 — Probate / Estate

**Psychology:** Seller (often an heir or executor) is dealing with grief, legal complexity, and a property they likely didn't ask to manage. They need patience, clarity, and someone who makes the process easy — not aggressive.

**SMS:**
> Hi [Name], I work with families managing estate properties and I came across [Address]. If there's ever a time it makes sense to talk about the property, I'm happy to help. No rush. — [Name]

**Email:**
> Subject: Estate property at [Address] — here if it's helpful
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I came across the property at [Address] through public records and wanted to reach out in case it's helpful.
>
> I specialize in working with families navigating estate properties. I know there's often a lot on your plate — legal process, family decisions, the property itself. I can make the real estate piece as simple and stress-free as possible.
>
> If and when the time is right, I'd be happy to talk. No pressure and no rush.
>
> [Name] | [Company] | [Phone]

**Direct Mail:**
> [Name],
>
> I understand you may be handling an estate that includes [Address]. Managing inherited property — especially while dealing with everything else involved — can be overwhelming.
>
> I work with families in exactly this situation. I can offer a fair cash offer, a quick close, or help you understand all your options. There's no obligation to move forward.
>
> If it would help to simply have a conversation, please call or text me at [Phone].
>
> [Name] | [Company]

### SITUATION 5 — Tax Delinquent / Code Violation

**Psychology:** Owner may be a tired/absentee landlord, financially overwhelmed, or unaware of the severity. They need someone who leads with a solution — not judgment.

**SMS:**
> Hi [Name], I came across your property at [Address] and wanted to reach out. I buy properties in as-is condition and can close quickly. Would you be open to a quick call? — [Name]

**Email:**
> Subject: Property at [Address] — quick question
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm a local investor and I came across your property at [Address]. I buy homes in as-is condition — no repairs required, no agents, no fees — and I can close on your timeline, sometimes in as little as 2 weeks.
>
> If you're open to it, I'd love to learn about your situation. There's no pressure or obligation.
>
> [Name] | [Company] | [Phone]

### SITUATION 6 — Absentee Owner / Tired Landlord

**Psychology:** Owner is likely an out-of-state investor or accidental landlord. They may be tired of tenants, repairs, or just want to liquidate. Lead with simplicity — "take the headache off your plate."

**SMS:**
> Hi [Name], I came across your rental at [Address]. If you've ever thought about selling — no tenants to deal with, no repairs, cash offer — I'd love to have a quick conversation. — [Name]

**Email:**
> Subject: Your rental at [Address] — would you ever consider selling?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed you own the rental property at [Address]. I work with landlords who are ready to exit — whether it's tenant headaches, deferred maintenance, or just time to move on.
>
> I can make you a cash offer, close on your timeline, and handle everything — including the tenants. No repairs, no showings, no agents in the middle.
>
> Would you be open to a quick call?
>
> [Name] | [Company] | [Phone]

**Direct Mail:**
> [Name],
>
> I'm a local real estate investor and I'd like to make you a cash offer for your rental property at [Address].
>
> I buy properties with tenants in place, in as-is condition, and on your timeline. No repairs. No showings. No agent commissions.
>
> If you've ever thought about selling — even just to explore what it might be worth — give me a call. No obligation.
>
> [Phone] | [Name] | [Company]

## Follow-Up Sequence (Non-Responders)

When a seller doesn't respond to first contact, use this cadence:

| Touch | Timing | Channel | Tone |
|-------|---------|---------|------|
| 1 | Day 1 | SMS or email | First outreach |
| 2 | Day 4 | Different channel | Short check-in, value-add |
| 3 | Day 10 | Voicemail | Empathetic, no pressure |
| 4 | Day 21 | Email or direct mail | "Still here when you're ready" |
| 5 | Day 45 | SMS | Re-open door, market update |
| 6 | Day 90 | Direct mail | Long-form letter, seasonal tie-in |

**Follow-up SMS (Touch 2):**
> Hi [Name], just following up on my note about [Address]. Still happy to chat if the timing works. No pressure. — [Name]

**Follow-up Voicemail (Touch 3):**
> "Hi [Name], [Your Name] again — I won't keep calling, I just wanted to leave one more message. I know timing is everything in real estate and I'm not in a rush. If you ever want to have a conversation about [Address], I'm here. [Phone]. Take care."

## Compliance Notes

- **SMS**: Obtain proper opt-in or confirm you have a prior business relationship before texting. TCPA compliance is critical — do not blast unscrubbed lists.
- **Direct Mail**: No restrictions on content, but fair housing laws apply.
- **Pre-Foreclosure outreach**: State laws vary. Some states restrict contact with homeowners in foreclosure. Verify local regulations before mailing/calling.
- **Do Not Call Registry**: Scrub lists before phone outreach.
- **Fair Housing**: Never include language that could be construed as targeting or discouraging based on race, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status, or other protected classes.
