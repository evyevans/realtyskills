# Off-Market Property Profile — detailed procedure

## Evidence register

For each property record identify source, access date, record date, parcel/property match, and whether it is an official record, third-party aggregation, owner statement, or observation. Prefer the applicable assessor, land registry/recorder, planning authority, and permitted court records. Correctly distinguish title records from tax-assessment records. Public availability does not grant permission to scrape, enrich, or repurpose personal data without considering local rules and service terms.

## Property and ownership review

Document permitted facts: property type, size, land-use classification, observed maintenance, recorded ownership name or entity where needed, transfer history, recorded liens or notices, and occupancy as confirmed. A vacancy observation does not prove abandonment. A filed notice does not prove a current legal status; check subsequent releases, dismissals, transfers, or recorded corrections. Do not label a person a squatter, insolvent, desperate, divorced, bereaved, elderly, or otherwise vulnerable from property records or appearance.

Check identity/parcel mismatches and obtain confirmation through authorized title or professional channels. Record “ownership/title confirmation pending” rather than certifying ownership from one record.

## Equity worksheet

Use a supported current-value range and dated payoff statements when available. Estimated net equity range = supported value range − confirmed payoff balances − other established obligations − estimated transaction costs. State inclusion and valuation dates. Original mortgage amounts do not establish current balances; assessed values do not establish market value. Without payoffs, provide the input worksheet, not a guessed equity percentage. Flag possible lien priority, probate, entity authority, and multiple-owner consent questions for qualified review.

## Diligence priorities

Prioritize verification by its effect on the proposed transaction: structural or safety concerns, uncertain title, occupancy/lease rights, unverified valuation, access restrictions, repairs, and financing. Use an evidence/confidence matrix for the property, not a numerical prediction of an owner's willingness to sell. Do not infer urgency from life events, neighborhood demographics, or protected characteristics.

## Outreach and handoff

Check permitted channels, opt-outs, existing representation, and applicable solicitation restrictions. Draft only when the contact basis supports it; otherwise return drafts on hold and a verification task. Explain the actual purpose and role of the sender. Ask whether the owner wishes to discuss options; do not claim knowledge of private financial trouble, imply affiliation with a court or government, invent a cash buyer, or pressure a response.

Provide a concise initial message for requested eligible channels, neutral answers to likely questions, and a stop rule after a decline or opt-out. Do not look up private contact details or send without the user's authorization.

## Deliverable

Return property fact sheet; evidence/status table; title and ownership discrepancies; conditional equity worksheet; condition/occupancy diligence checklist; transaction constraints; priority questions for owner/title professional/inspector; and outreach drafts or a contact-basis hold. Keep personal data out of shareable versions unless required.

## Before delivery

No inferred personal distress, motivation score, demographic proxy, fabricated balance, or claimed ownership certification. Every flag has evidence, status, and a next verification action.
