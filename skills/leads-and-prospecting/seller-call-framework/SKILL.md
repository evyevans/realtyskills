# Seller Call Framework

A battle-tested call structure for every seller scenario — from the first inbound call to the negotiation table. The goal is never to "sell" a seller. It's to understand their situation well enough that the right solution presents itself naturally.

## When to Use

- Prepping for a first call with a motivated seller (investor/wholesaler)
- Preparing for a listing appointment call (agent)
- Running a live negotiation or price reduction conversation
- Handling objections during the seller conversation
- Training new acquisitions or listing agents on call structure

---

## The Core Principle

**Talk less. Ask more.** The seller who feels heard will move. The seller who feels pitched will hang up. Your job on any seller call is to understand four things: motivation, timeline, condition, and price expectation. Everything else follows from those four.

---

## FRAMEWORK 1 — First Inbound Call (Investor / Wholesaler)

Use when a motivated seller calls you back from outreach.

### Opening (30 seconds)
> "Hi, is this [Seller Name]? Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. Thanks for calling me back — I know you're probably getting a lot of calls. I just want to have a real conversation and understand your situation. Is now a good time to talk for a few minutes?"

*If they say "what are you, a real estate agent?" — respond:*
> "I'm actually a local investor. I buy homes directly — no listing on the market, no showings, just a straightforward offer if it makes sense for both of us. Totally up to you."

---

### Qualification — The 5 Core Questions

Ask these in a conversational way. Not rapid-fire. Let them talk.

**1. Tell me about the property.**
> "So tell me a little about the house. How long have you owned it?"

*Listen for: tenure, emotional attachment, renovation history*

**2. What's prompting you to think about selling?**
> "What's making you consider selling now? Or what would need to happen for you to make a move?"

*Listen for: urgency level, true motivation — job, divorce, estate, financial, relocation, tired landlord*

**3. What condition is it in?**
> "Has the home been kept up, or would you say it needs some work? I ask because it affects what I'd be able to offer."

*Listen for: deferred maintenance, structural issues, tenant damage, cosmetic vs. major repairs*

**4. What's your timeline?**
> "Is there a specific date you're working toward, or are you more flexible?"

*Listen for: urgency — foreclosure deadline, probate timeline, life event*

**5. What are you hoping to walk away with?**
> "I don't want to waste your time, so let me ask — do you have a number in mind for what you'd need to net from the sale?"

*This surfaces price expectation. If they won't give a number:*
> "That's totally fine — I'll put together some numbers and we can go from there. I just don't want to come back with something that doesn't work for you."

---

### Making the Offer (Investor / Wholesaler)

Once you have the four data points, transition to the offer:

> "Okay, [Name], I appreciate you sharing all of that. Based on what you've told me, and assuming the condition is roughly what you described, I think I'd be able to come in somewhere around [OFFER RANGE]. I want to be upfront — I'm not a retail buyer, so my number isn't going to be full market value. What I can offer is a simple, certain close on your timeline with no repairs, no showings, and no agent fees. How does that sound as a starting point?"

**If they balk at the number:**
> "I understand — that may not be where you need to be. Can I ask, what would need to be true for a number like that to work? I want to see if there's a way to bridge the gap, whether that's price, timeline, or something else."

**If they say they'll think about it:**
> "Totally fair. Can I ask — is it mainly the price that you need to think about, or is there something else going on? I want to make sure I'm not missing something. And when would be a good time to follow up?"

---

### Closing / Next Step

Every call ends with a clear next step. Never let it end with "I'll think about it" and nothing scheduled.

> "Here's what I'd like to do — let me put together a formal offer in writing and send it over to you by [Date]. Then we can get on a quick call to review it together. Does that work?"

OR (if they're ready to move):

> "Can we set up a time for me to come walk through the property? That way I can put together the best offer possible. I have time [Day] at [Time] or [Day] at [Time] — which works better for you?"

## FRAMEWORK 2 — Listing Appointment Call (Agent)

Use when an agent is calling to pre-qualify or prep a seller before the in-person listing appointment.

### Opening:
> "Hi [Name], this is [Agent] with [Brokerage]. Thanks for reaching out about selling your home at [Address]. I have my appointment scheduled for [Date/Time] and I just wanted to connect briefly beforehand so I can make sure I'm as prepared as possible for you. Is now okay?"

### Pre-Appointment Questions:
1. "How long have you owned the home, and what was your original purchase price?" *(sets baseline)*
2. "Have you done any significant updates or renovations since buying?" *(helps with CMA)*
3. "Do you have a target date you'd like to be closed by?" *(reveals urgency)*
4. "Have you had any other agents out to the property?" *(are you competing?)*
5. "Is there a price you have in mind, or are you looking to me to give you that guidance?" *(anchors conversation)*

### Pre-Qualifying Their Motivation:
> "I want to make sure I'm the right fit for you. Can I ask — what matters most to you in choosing an agent? Is it price, speed, communication, or something else?"

---

## FRAMEWORK 3 — Price Reduction Conversation

Use after a listing has sat on the market without activity.

### Do Not Lead With "We Need to Reduce the Price"

Instead:
> "[Name], I want to have an honest conversation with you because I believe in being straight with my clients. The market has given us feedback over the last [X] days, and I think it's worth us sitting down to review what that data is telling us."

### Present the Data:
> "Here's what I'm seeing — [X] homes in your price range have gone under contract in the last 30 days. The average list-to-close time for those is [Y] days. Homes priced above the market are sitting [Z] days on average. Your home has been active for [days]. The showings we've had have been [low/moderate], and the feedback has been consistent: [feedback theme]."

### The Price Reduction Framing:
> "I'm not recommending a price cut because I want a quick close. I'm recommending it because the math is clear — every week on market without a contract costs you carrying costs, negotiating leverage, and buyer confidence. A [X%] adjustment puts you directly in the zone where buyers are actively making offers. That's [$ amount]. What are your thoughts?"

### If They Resist:
> "I hear you — and I want to honor your equity. Here's what I'd propose: let's give it [2 more weeks] at the current price, but let's agree today that if we don't get [an offer / X showings] by [date], we revisit the number. Fair?"

## Common Seller Objections on the Call

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Your offer is too low." | "I understand — can you help me understand what you're basing your number on? I want to see if we're looking at the same comparable sales." |
| "I'm not in a rush." | "That's great — timeline flexibility is actually something I can work with. Can I ask, if you weren't in a rush, what would make selling attractive to you right now?" |
| "I need to talk to my spouse/partner." | "Of course — that makes sense. Would it be helpful if I joined that conversation? Sometimes having me there to answer questions makes it easier. Or when do you think you'll be able to chat with them?" |
| "I had another offer for more." | "I respect that. Was that offer from a cash buyer or financed? And did it come with proof of funds or a pre-approval? I want to make sure you're comparing apples to apples." |
| "I want to try selling it myself first." | "That's a fair approach. Can I ask — what's your plan if it doesn't sell in [30/60] days? I just want to understand your backup." |
| "I don't want to pay commission." | "I completely understand. Let me ask — what's more important to you: paying zero commission, or walking away with the most money possible? Sometimes those are the same thing, and sometimes they're not." |

## Notes for Acquisitions Managers / Team Leads

When using this framework with a team:
- Role-play the qualification section weekly — the questions feel natural with practice, not script-read
- Record calls (with consent where required) and debrief: what did the seller say that revealed true motivation?
- The goal of the first call is NOT to make the offer — it's to set the appointment or get enough info to make a credible offer. Don't rush.
- If a seller won't answer motivation questions, they're not a motivated seller yet. Nurture and follow up — don't force it.
