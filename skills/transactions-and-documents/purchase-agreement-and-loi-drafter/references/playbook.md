# Purchase Agreement and LOI Draft — detailed procedure

## Document choice and intake

Clarify whether the user wants an LOI, negotiation term sheet, or form-based purchase agreement; infer it if the request is clear. Confirm intended audience, transaction type, jurisdiction, legal property description or source to obtain it, party/entity identifiers and signing authority, and any approved form/version. Addresses alone may be insufficient for legal property identification; flag this rather than assuming a substitute is valid.

Ask for price/currency, payment structure, deposit amount and due event, escrow holder, financing details, due-diligence conditions, title review, target closing and possession, cost allocation, included/excluded fixtures or goods, expiry if intended, and specific requested provisions. Do not insert a generic business-day period, offer expiry hour, standard commission, default remedy, or interest rate.

## LOI and term-sheet mode

Provide a coherent draft with parties/property, proposed economics, diligence and financing conditions, expected closing/possession, open issues, intended binding/nonbinding status, and any user-requested signature/acknowledgment fields. Do not claim an LOI is wholly nonbinding by label alone: confidentiality, exclusivity, deposits, expenses, or other provisions can create obligations. Clearly segregate any proposed binding provisions and identify counsel-review questions. Leave unsupported terms explicitly unresolved.

## Purchase-agreement mode

Use the authorized current local form if available. Populate confirmed fields and preserve existing form text. Provide proposed custom provisions in a separate change log. Without an appropriate form, deliver a complete transaction term sheet and draft-for-review clause outline; do not manufacture a purported execution-ready local contract.

Examine parties, property, price/funds, escrow/deposit release, inspection/diligence, financing/appraisal, title/survey, mandatory disclosures, settlement/possession, cost allocation, notices, default/remedies, assignment, and execution as applicable. No automatic assignment right, seller-financing clause, “subject-to” provision, financing waiver for a cash label, or attorney-review period. Requested complex financing must identify unresolved lender-consent, security-document, liability, and local-law questions rather than imply a generic clause resolves them.

## Reconciliation and delivery

Check amounts against the price, deposits and credits without double-counting, dates against triggering events, consistent entity/party names, defined terms, and attachment references. Verify relevant local official requirements when possible; never rely on a memorized list of “attorney-review states.” Identify changes to form text, party agreement still needed, and professional review.

Deliver a complete review draft reflecting the available facts, a deal-summary table, missing/negotiable terms, proposed clause log, binding-status questions, and an execution checklist. Signature placeholders and unsupplied factual terms must remain visibly unresolved. Sending for signature or issuing an offer requires the user's actual authorization and relevant tool; the agent cannot sign or accept for a party.

## Before delivery

No fabricated legal description, dates, default remedies, assignment permissions, disclosure waivers, or enforceability guarantee. Binding status is evaluated by actual terms; draft-only fields remain marked.
