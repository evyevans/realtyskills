# Representation Agreement Draft — detailed procedure

## Source and authority

Confirm listing versus buyer representation, residential/commercial or other scope, exclusive/nonexclusive terms as requested, jurisdiction, brokerage, agency relationship, and intended audience. Use the user's licensed or otherwise authorized current form. Identify form title/version and any incorporated schedules. Do not retrieve restricted forms or assume all jurisdictions use U.S. association agreements. Without a suitable form, deliver a complete structured term sheet and proposed clause questions rather than a fabricated legally valid contract.

## Term mapping

Create a field table: form section, user-confirmed term, source, and unresolved item. Include legal party/entity names where required, signatory authority, property or search scope, start/end event or date, services, permitted marketing, cancellation, protection/carryover provisions, expenses, agency/disclosure acknowledgments, and signature fields. Use placeholders only for real gaps; do not silently insert terms to make the document appear complete.

Compensation must reflect the user's actual negotiated terms: amount or objectively determinable calculation, basis, payment event, payer, permitted credits, expenses, limitations, and any applicable disclosure. Do not impose a standard commission, minimum, cap, term, or compensation arrangement. For affected U.S. MLS participants, verify current written-buyer-agreement rules and local forms; association rules and state law are distinct and neither is a universal global requirement.

## Draft and review

Populate the form without removing mandatory text or protections. For requested nonstandard provisions, provide exact proposed language separately, the intended effect, affected form section, potential conflicts, and a review question. Do not default to arbitration, attorneys' fees, dual agency, an automatic carryover period, guaranteed sale, or unilateral cancellation. Do not claim enforceability or completed compliance.

Compare supplied form versus draft using a tracked-change tool if available; otherwise provide a side-by-side original/proposed/change-reason table and disclose that it is not a native redline. Reconcile conflicting compensation clauses, service promises, dates, definitions, notices, and incorporated attachments.

## Deliverable

Return scope/form/version statement; populated review draft or full term sheet; field-status table; change log; unresolved terms; agency and compensation disclosure questions; concise client explanation of services, term, payment, and termination; and prioritized broker/attorney review tasks. Keep unsupplied identifiers and signature dates unfilled and clearly marked. Do not sign, send for signature, or issue an agreement unless separately authorized; never act as a party's signatory.

## Before delivery

Check form version, permission, required attachments, term consistency, compensation math, legal party/signature identity, and source-backed disclosure requirements. No standard commission or automatic legal clauses.

## Official starting points

[NAR written buyer agreements](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101) and [negotiating buyer agreements](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-negotiating-written-buyer-agreements), only for their applicable U.S. membership/MLS context. Verify the relevant licensing authority and brokerage-approved form locally.

These are jurisdiction-specific starting points, not universal rules or proof of current applicability. Open and verify the relevant source during use; choose the corresponding local authority elsewhere.
