# Comparative Market Analysis — detailed procedure

## Intake and evidence selection

Confirm purpose (listing discussion, acquisition screening, or another use), effective date, market boundary, property type, ownership or tenure, measurement standard, condition, amenities, and known restrictions. Use an alias unless identification is necessary. Check unit consistency and conflicting assessor, listing, and owner records. Do not resolve discrepancies by silently choosing a number.

For each candidate comp collect source, status, contract/closing dates if available, actual sale price, concessions if documented, area, condition, relevant features, location context, and data gaps. Prefer competing submarkets and similar use/tenure. Choose the search window and geographic scope from local transaction density and comparability; do not enforce a universal radius, comp count, or age cutoff. Explain any expansion. Active asking prices describe competition; undisclosed pending prices do not establish closed-sale value.

## Adjust and calculate

Create an adjustment grid: comp, feature difference, evidence for rate or amount, signed adjustment, and verification status. Adjust the comp toward the subject: add for a supported subject advantage and subtract for a disadvantage. Account for condition, concessions, market movement, and features without double-counting correlated differences. Use matched-market or other supported local evidence. Cost of an improvement is not automatically its contributory value. Do not default to fixed bedroom, garage, renovation, or pool premiums.

Adjusted sale price = recorded sale price + supported signed adjustments. Show gross absolute adjustments as well as net adjustment; large offsetting items can hide weak comparability. Do not apply arbitrary disqualification percentages. If adjustment evidence is missing, retain a qualitative comparison and state its effect on confidence.

Price per unit of area = sale price / that comp's comparable measured area. If an adjustment grid places comps on the subject's basis, label adjusted price / subject area explicitly; do not mix denominators. Use ratios as cross-checks, not whole-property adjustment rates. For a weighted result, state the evidence-based weights, normalize them, and calculate sum(weight × adjusted price) / sum(weights). If weights are not supportable, show unweighted statistics and qualitative reconciliation.

## Market context and reconciliation

Calculate sale/list ratios only with a defined original or final list-price field. Calculate months of supply as active inventory divided by average monthly closed sales over a stated period, with matching geography, segment, and status definitions. Zero sales makes the ratio undefined. Explain medians, sample size, seasonality, and differing periods; no universal inventory or days-on-market threshold establishes a market type.

Reconcile relevant sales, adjustments, and current alternatives into a supported range. Explain how asking-price options relate to that range and seller goals. Do not invent offer probabilities, expected marketing time, an automatic premium, or guaranteed proceeds. If evidence is inadequate, deliver the comp-research plan and full subject/meeting materials with pricing pending.

## Deliverable

Provide executive pricing brief; subject fact table; accepted and excluded comp table with reasons; signed adjustment grid or qualitative difference matrix; calculation summary; market context; supported range and conditional listing options; plain-language seller explanation; and evidence gaps with owners. A CMA is an agent analysis, not a certified appraisal.

## Before delivery

Check sale status, units, dates, concessions, adjustment signs, denominator consistency, weight totals, and source traceability. No numeric price range when the evidence cannot support one.

## Official starting points

[Fannie Mae comparable-sales selection](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-1.3-08/comparable-sales) and [market-supported adjustments](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-1.3-09/adjustments-comparable-sales). These are U.S. mortgage appraisal references, not a global CMA mandate.

These are jurisdiction-specific starting points, not universal rules or proof of current applicability. Open and verify the relevant source during use; choose the corresponding local authority elsewhere.
