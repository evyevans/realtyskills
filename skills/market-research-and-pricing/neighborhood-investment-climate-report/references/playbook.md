# Neighborhood Investment Report — detailed procedure

## Boundary and evidence

Use a defined map/administrative or user-specified boundary and distinguish overlapping statistical areas. Record dataset geography, period, segment, source, publication/retrieval date, and sample size. Sales, asking rents, executed rents, and occupancy evidence are different measures. Avoid implying a neighborhood-wide investment conclusion from a few selected listings.

## Investment dimensions

Review relevant transaction/liquidity evidence, achieved/asking rents with distinctions, operating costs, insurance/tax exposure, vacancy/lease-up, supply pipeline, infrastructure access, land-use/zoning, permits, building condition risks, flood/fire or other applicable hazards, and rental/licensing restrictions. Verify planned projects' official status, funding, approval, and timing rather than treat announcements as completed development.

Do not score desirability, safety, tenant quality, or investment return from race, national origin, religion, family status, disability, income demographics, or other protected-trait proxies. Do not assign “gentrification stages” from demographic change or suggest displacement as a strategy. If displacement or tenant-rights impacts are relevant, describe documented obligations and operational/ethical considerations without targeting vulnerable residents.

## Strategy and calculations

Compare the requested rental, renovation/resale, commercial, development, or other strategy to sourced demand, permitted use, financing/capital needs, operating expenses, transaction costs, and exit liquidity. A neighborhood report does not underwrite an individual property. Refer to specific property evidence needed for taxes, insurance, leases, title, inspections, and costs before a recommendation.

If requested, use an explicit user-approved comparison rubric for objective strategy criteria. Show each criterion, source, uncertainty, and weighting; missing evidence remains unknown. Do not generate a pseudo-scientific investment climate score, universal cap-rate target, or unsupported appreciation forecast.

Use scenario language for changes in supply, rents, financing, hazard exposure, or regulation. Official zoning classification does not guarantee a particular development entitlement; verify parcel-specific restrictions and approval requirements. Infrastructure proximity does not prove future rent growth.

## Deliverable

Return boundary/strategy scope; evidence freshness/coverage; market and operating conditions; infrastructure/project status; regulatory/use and physical-risk constraints; strengths/tradeoffs tied to the strategy; supported scenarios; property-level due diligence; and sourced conclusions with limitations. Where evidence is thin, provide a research-backed partial report and the missing-data plan instead of fictional ratings.

## Before delivery

No demographic steering, inferred tenant quality, automatic gentrification stage, or promised appreciation. Boundary/period differences are explicit, projects are labeled by actual status, and parcel-level permissions remain unverified until checked.
