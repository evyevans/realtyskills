# Rental Property Evaluation — detailed procedure

## Income and operating expenses

Confirm property/unit mix, leased versus market rents, lease/collection evidence, reporting period, occupancy, concessions, bad debt, and ancillary income. Annualize only when the input period is representative; identify seasonal or short-term operations. Gross potential rent is rent at the specified occupancy/rent basis before losses. Effective gross income = potential rent − distinct vacancy/concession/credit losses + other income. If using actual collections, do not subtract the same loss again.

Use actual taxes, insurance, management, routine repairs, utilities, association costs, payroll, administration, and other property operating expenses. Evaluate reassessment, expiring concessions, and owner-paid versus tenant-paid items from evidence. Missing costs are unknown, not zero. Do not default to property-type vacancy rates or percentage repair/insurance assumptions.

NOI = effective gross income − operating expenses, excluding debt service, income tax, depreciation, and capital expenditures. Show replacement reserves separately; where a lender uses an adjusted net cash-flow definition, label it and reconcile to NOI. Routine repair and capital replacement are distinct. Self-management may have an optional economic-cost scenario, not an invented paid expense.

## Financing and cash requirements

Record actual loan amount, annual rate/rate convention, payment frequency, amortization, term/maturity, interest-only periods, fees, and any balloon. Standard fixed fully amortizing payment per period = L × r / (1 − (1+r)^(-n)), with periodic rate r and n amortization payments; for r=0, payment=L/n. Do not assume a mortgage always has a monthly rate equal to nominal annual rate/12; use lender terms. Term can end before amortization and create a balloon. Use a verified amortization schedule or code.

Initial cash requirement = purchase + closing/acquisition costs + immediate capital work + funded reserves − loan proceeds − valid credits, with deposits already paid counted once. Track future equity contributions separately. Financing fees may be funded or paid upfront; reconcile sources and uses.

## Metrics and projections

Cap rate = NOI / price or specified value basis; name that denominator. DSCR = the named lender/analysis income numerator / debt service for the same period; no debt makes it not applicable. Gross rent multiplier = price / annual gross rent, if useful. Pre-tax equity cash flow = NOI − debt service − capital spending − reserve funding + reserve releases, avoiding double-counting reserve-funded work. Define any lender-adjusted cash measure separately.

Cash-on-cash = relevant-period pre-tax cash flow / stated contributed-equity basis. Explain additional capital calls and timing. Do not apply universal cap-rate, DSCR, per-unit cash-flow, or return thresholds; compare to verified lender requirements and investor goals.

Build the requested hold period from explicit rent/expense growth, vacancy, capital timing, financing changes, and exit assumptions. No automatic appreciation. Exit value requires a stated supported method (such as selected forward NOI / exit cap rate). Net equity proceeds subtract selling costs and actual modeled loan payoff. Value without a sale is unrealized equity, not a cash distribution. Compute dated returns only with complete dated flows; disclose exclusions and uncertainty.

## Deliverable

Provide input/source register; income and expenses with missing fields; NOI/reserve reconciliation; debt schedule; initial and later cash needs; labeled metrics; period-by-period cash flow; net exit or unrealized-equity scenario as requested; user-approved sensitivity cases; and decision conditions. No unconditional investment or lender-approval claim.

## Before delivery

Income periods and units match; loss, reserves, repairs, CapEx, deposits, fees, and principal are counted once; loan assumptions are actual; exit proceeds are net; zero denominators are marked undefined/not applicable.
