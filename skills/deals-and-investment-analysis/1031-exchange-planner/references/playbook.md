# 1031 Exchange Planning Playbook

Use this playbook to organize facts and prepare a planning brief for a potential U.S. Internal Revenue Code Section 1031 exchange. It is a transaction-coordination aid, not tax or legal advice and not a qualification opinion.

The framework is U.S.-specific because Section 1031 is a U.S. federal tax provision. Do not translate its rules into another country's law. If the taxpayer, property, or intended transaction is outside the U.S. federal framework, explain that limitation and direct the user to a qualified local tax professional.

## 1. Establish scope and safe tool use

1. Identify the user’s role: taxpayer, agent, transaction coordinator, attorney, CPA, or other participant. Do not assume the user is the taxpayer or authorized to disclose another person’s financial records.
2. Confirm the taxpayer/entity, relinquished property use, property location, replacement property locations, transfer stage, and transaction type. Flag mixed personal and investment use, property held primarily for sale, foreign property, related parties, partnerships or other entity changes, reverse exchanges, improvement exchanges, multiple-property exchanges, and prior exchanges for professional review.
3. Inspect the tools available in the current agent environment. If current web search or browsing is available, retrieve current official IRS materials. Use only connectors relevant to the task and authorized by the user. A connection to a transaction platform, calendar, drive, or CRM does not by itself authorize reading unrelated records or making changes.
4. For missing tools, ask the user to provide relevant facts or redacted records. Never pretend to have queried a connector, verified a document, or checked current law when that did not happen.
5. Keep all work read-only unless the user explicitly instructs a particular external action and the required connector is available. Drafting a written identification for QI review is distinct from sending it or submitting it.

## 2. Collect the facts that control the review

Ask for the material inputs in one focused request. Mark each as confirmed, user-reported, document-verified, professional-verified, or unknown.

| Fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Date the relinquished property was transferred, with supporting record if available | Starting point for the identification and exchange periods |
| Contract, closing, and deed/transfer dates if they differ | Helps confirm which event controls; do not substitute a contract or listing date without verification |
| Taxpayer and ownership/entity structure, using aliases where possible | Entity, partnership, related-party, or ownership changes can alter the analysis |
| Property type, use, location, and holding purpose | Helps identify potential scope, like-kind, mixed-use, and jurisdiction issues |
| QI or other exchange arrangement, agreement date, and who controls proceeds | Helps surface actual/constructive receipt and safe-harbor questions |
| Sale/transfer records, proceeds, liabilities, transaction expenses, and any non-like-kind property | Facts for the professional's exchange and potential boot analysis |
| Adjusted basis, depreciation, and other tax attributes, if supplied by the CPA | Needed for tax reporting and gain analysis; do not infer or reconstruct without adequate records |
| Candidate replacement properties, their locations, values, intended use, ownership, and status | Supports an identification-rule worksheet and feasibility discussion |
| Relevant federal filing status, return due date, extensions, and applicable IRS relief | May affect the outer exchange deadline |
| User's intended decision, audience, and desired deliverable | Keeps the brief useful to an agent, investor, or professional handoff |

Do not request sensitive details that are unnecessary for the requested deliverable. Encourage redaction of taxpayer identification numbers, bank details, full account numbers, signatures, and unrelated personal information.

## 3. Verify the law and transaction-specific guidance

When browsing is available, consult current primary IRS material. Start with:

- [IRS Publication 544, Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544)
- [IRS Instructions for Form 8824, Like-Kind Exchanges](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824)
- Any applicable current IRS regulation, revenue procedure, or official disaster-relief notice referenced by those materials.

Use the current tax-year instructions when available. Record the source title, link, tax-year/version, and access date in the deliverable. Prefer the applicable official source over secondary summaries. If sources appear inconsistent, quote or describe the conflict briefly and ask the tax professional to resolve it. Do not silently pick the convenient rule.

If current verification is unavailable, say so clearly. You may organize user-provided facts and explain the commonly applicable framework at a high level, but label rules and calculated deadlines as unverified and avoid a conclusion that the exchange qualifies.

## 4. Build a provisional timeline

Only calculate deadlines after the transfer date is confirmed. Use calendar-day arithmetic and state the counting convention. The periods generally run from the transfer of the relinquished property; they do not start from the date a replacement property is identified.

- **Identification period:** replacement property generally must be identified no later than 45 days after transfer of the relinquished property.
- **Exchange period:** replacement property generally must be received by the earlier of 180 days after that transfer or the due date (including extensions) of the taxpayer’s federal return for the tax year in which the relinquished property was transferred.
- **Relief and special cases:** check current official guidance for any disaster postponement or other relief that may apply to the taxpayer and transaction. Do not claim that extensions or exceptions never exist.

Show the input transfer date, day-count convention, provisional 45-day date, provisional 180-day date, applicable return due date if known, which outer limit appears earlier, and the source used. If the return due date or transfer date is uncertain, show the unresolved input and do not manufacture a final deadline. Recommend that the taxpayer and QI confirm every operational deadline in writing.

## 5. Explain identification rules without choosing for the taxpayer

For a deferred exchange, summarize the identification options using current IRS material and flag any detailed conditions for QI or tax counsel confirmation:

1. **Three-property rule:** generally permits identifying up to three replacement properties without regard to their value.
2. **200-percent rule:** generally permits identifying any number of replacement properties if their aggregate fair market value does not exceed 200% of the aggregate fair market value of the relinquished property or properties, measured as required by the applicable rule.
3. **95-percent exception:** if neither of the first two conditions is met, a special exception generally requires acquiring replacement property with aggregate fair market value of at least 95% of the aggregate fair market value of all identified replacement properties.

Do not recommend a rule based only on the number of properties. Ask the user which properties they may identify, collect values and valuation dates from reliable sources, show a transparent comparison, and label unresolved valuation or legal issues. Do not draft or submit a final identification notice without explicit instruction and professional review.

## 6. Organize value, liability, and potential boot issues

Do not use a simplistic formula that treats the difference between sale price and replacement price, or the difference between old and new mortgages, as the final taxable boot or tax bill. The exchange treatment can depend on money or non-like-kind property received, liabilities transferred or assumed, exchange expenses, basis, depreciation, transaction structure, and other facts.

Create a fact table from records, preserving source and status for each figure. If the user asks for calculations, perform only transparent arithmetic the facts support, label it as a worksheet, and do not convert it into a recognized-gain or tax-liability conclusion. Ask the CPA or attorney to determine:

- whether a value or liability difference is treated as money or other property received in this transaction;
- the amount and character of any recognized gain and any depreciation-related treatment;
- the adjusted basis, exchange expenses, and tax attributes used for reporting;
- whether the exchange satisfies current federal and state requirements; and
- whether the taxpayer's filing deadline, extensions, or relief affect the exchange period.

Do not assume a particular capital-gains rate, depreciation-recapture rate, state treatment, net investment income tax, or taxpayer bracket. Do not estimate a tax bill without complete professional-verified assumptions; even then, present any arithmetic as a CPA-review worksheet rather than advice.

## 7. Surface QI, timing, and execution questions

A QI is commonly used as a safe harbor in deferred exchanges, but do not state that a QI is universally required in every exchange structure. IRS deferred-exchange safe-harbor rules are technical. Flag any actual or constructive receipt of proceeds, missing written exchange agreement, potential disqualified-person relationship, or late QI engagement for prompt professional review. Do not tell the user an exchange is automatically disqualified or that funds must be returned without current source verification and transaction-specific professional advice.

Prepare a checklist of questions, as applicable:

- Has the taxpayer's QI or exchange counsel confirmed the transfer date and written exchange agreement?
- Who holds or controls proceeds, and could any arrangement create actual or constructive receipt?
- Who may be a disqualified person under the facts?
- What form of written identification, delivery method, and receipt deadline does the QI require?
- Are all candidates described unambiguously, and are values and valuation dates documented?
- Do related-party, reverse, improvement, mixed-use, multi-asset, or entity issues require separate advice?
- Which filing deadline and any current relief control the exchange-period end date?

Do not recommend named QI companies or other vendors. If the user asks for provider research, conduct a separate current comparison using their location and criteria.

## 8. Compare candidate replacement properties neutrally

If candidate details are provided, make a neutral table. Do not label a candidate “recommended,” “safe,” or “qualifying.” For each candidate, show only supported facts and flags:

| Candidate | Confirmed description | Reported fair market value and source/date | Ownership/use questions | Financing/liability facts | Location/scope flags | Timing/status | Open questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not repeat a property's private address in a shareable brief unless the user requests it. Use an alias such as “Candidate A” and place sensitive details in a separate private worksheet if needed.

## Quality check before delivery

- Confirm the skill's U.S. scope and the user’s role, property use, and transaction structure.
- Confirm the controlling transfer date from a reliable source; do not substitute a stale example date.
- Recheck all date arithmetic, counting convention, tax-return deadline, and applicable official relief.
- Verify current IRS material or disclose that current verification was unavailable.
- Distinguish recorded facts, user-reported facts, assumptions, and unknowns.
- Compare identification rules using the correct valuation basis; do not pick a rule for the taxpayer.
- Do not equate sale/replacement price differences or debt differences directly with taxable boot or tax due.
- Do not include vendor names, specific locations, example client names, invented amounts, or stale dates in a reusable deliverable.
- Include practical next steps and focused questions for the QI, CPA, and attorney.
- Keep external actions and transaction changes behind explicit user instruction.
