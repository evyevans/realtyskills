# Wholesale Deal Analysis — detailed procedure

## Property and transaction baseline

Identify the interest being acquired and marketed (property ownership, assignable contract interest, or another structure), current contract price, deposit, assignment restrictions, expiration and closing terms, occupancy, and known title/condition issues. Verify jurisdiction-specific licensing, registration, disclosure, advertising, and assignment rules with the relevant regulator or counsel. Do not assume an investor can assign any contract or advertise ownership they do not hold.

## Valuation and repairs

Validate sold comparables by property type, completed condition, location, date, size, and concessions. Keep resale asking prices separate. Reconcile ARV as a supported range, not a guaranteed exit price. Do not obtain restricted MLS data without access or infer interior condition from exterior photos.

Build a trade-level repair schedule: item, quantity/unit, quote or evidence, estimate status, contingency basis, permit/inspection dependencies, and completion assumptions. Obtain current local estimates for material gaps. Do not insert universal renovation prices. Distinguish essential safety/structural work from elective upgrades and identify specialist inspections.

## Economics and offer calculation

For the end buyer, total project cost includes acquisition, buyer closing costs, repairs, financing fees and interest, holding costs, selling costs, and any assignment fee paid by that buyer. Profit before tax = supported net resale proceeds minus those costs. Loan principal is funding, not a second purchase expense; track repayment in the cash waterfall.

A maximum-offer worksheet may solve: resale proceeds − repairs − non-purchase costs − desired buyer profit − assignment fee = maximum contract purchase price. State whether costs vary with purchase or resale price; solve consistently instead of subtracting both a percentage cost and its already-included amount. The “70% rule” is only an optional user-requested heuristic, not a valuation method or universal underwriting standard. Compare it to the itemized model and explain discrepancies.

Wholesaler net = assignment revenue minus wholesaler-specific marketing, diligence, transaction, financing, and other attributable costs. Show buyer price, contract price, gross fee, and costs separately. A double close requires its own two-closing costs, funding availability, and legal review; it is not economically identical to an assignment.

## Exits and capital

Compare only exits requested and supported: assignment, resale after renovation, resale with limited work, rental hold, or refinance-and-hold. Rental alternatives require rents, operating expenses, reserves, debt terms, and a separate cash-flow model. Refinancing proceeds and feasibility are conditional lender assumptions; never assume the investor recovers all equity.

Create a sources-and-uses schedule and a time-phased liquidity estimate, including deposit, repair draws, interest timing, contingency, and reserves. Separate committed funds from hoped-for financing. Stress-test lower resale value, higher repair costs, and delayed exit using user-approved ranges; identify the break-even price and assumptions driving viability.

## Deliverable

Return property/contract baseline; ARV evidence table; repair schedule; sources/uses and cash timing; itemized offer worksheet; buyer and wholesaler profit tables; requested exit comparison; base/downside scenarios; title, condition, financing, and regulatory diligence list; and a conditional recommendation against the user's stated targets. Add a seller-facing factual offer summary if requested, without promising closing or disguising assignment economics.

## Before delivery

Check that the assignment fee appears once in each relevant party’s economics; purchase and loan principal are not double-counted; funding availability is distinguished from profit; no contract rights, seller motivation, ARV, or legal permission is invented.
