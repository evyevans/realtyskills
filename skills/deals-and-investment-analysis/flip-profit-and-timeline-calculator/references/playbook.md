# Fix and Flip Analysis — detailed procedure

## Validate the project

Confirm asset type, market, currency, size, occupancy/access, acquisition price, current contract status, renovation standard, permits, contractor availability, and evidence for expected resale. Separate quotes from allowances, lender commitments from estimates, and proposed milestones from signed obligations. Photos cannot establish concealed defects or permit approval. Do not default to a particular rate, points charge, project length, contingency, resale commission, or appreciation rate.

## Budget and financing

Separate purchase costs, acquisition closing/transfer costs, renovation trade items, permits/design, contingency, taxes/insurance/utilities/association costs, lender fees, interest, sale costs, and any relevant extension or early-repayment charges. Distinguish refundable deposits from expenses and reserves from spent costs.

For each loan record principal, rate convention, term, interest-only or amortizing schedule, draw timing, unused-funds fees, points basis, financing of fees, maturity, extension terms, and payoff provisions. Calculate interest on the actual balance and period under the supplied convention. Do not charge interest on an entire repair facility before it is drawn unless the terms require it. If terms are missing, show the financing worksheet and conditional results.

## Cash flow and returns

Project profit before tax = gross resale price − selling costs − acquisition price − acquisition expenses − renovation − carrying expenses − financing expenses. Show which items are already included to avoid duplicates. Loan proceeds/principal repayment are cash-flow movements; they are not additional profit expenses.

Build a time-phased cash ledger of equity contributions, debt draws, expenditures, debt payments, and sale proceeds. Net cash to equity at exit = sale proceeds after selling costs and all payoffs. Reconcile total equity inflows minus contributions to project profit under the stated scope.

ROI must name its denominator: project profit / total project cost, or profit attributable to equity / equity contributed, as requested. Show both only if useful. Annualized returns based on a single initial/final cash flow differ from dated IRR when contributions occur throughout construction. Use a calculator/code for dated cash flows; do not estimate IRR from simple profit percentage. Mark undefined metrics when denominator or time is zero.

Break-even resale price with a percentage selling-cost rate s = (purchase + acquisition costs + renovation + carrying/financing costs + fixed selling costs) / (1 − s), assuming other costs do not vary with sale price. State assumptions; use a consistent solver for price-linked costs. Taxes on gain are excluded unless complete professional-verified tax assumptions are supplied.

## Timeline and downside cases

Build dependencies from acquisition/access → design/permits → procurement → work phases → inspections/punch list → marketing → buyer diligence/financing → closing. Use contractor/lender/user lead times, actual local holidays and time zone where necessary. Identify long-lead materials, occupancy, weather dependencies, and overlapping tasks. Unknown durations remain planning assumptions, not promised completion dates.

Compare base and user-approved stress cases for resale reduction, repair overrun, delayed exit, and funding cost change. Recalculate carrying costs for delays rather than adding a generic penalty. Show peak cash requirement, negative cash-flow periods, margin erosion, and whether each case meets the user's targets.

## Deliverable

Return assumptions/evidence register, budget with sources, financing ledger, periodic cash schedule, profit reconciliation, labeled ROI/IRR where calculable, break-even analysis, milestone/owner/dependency table, sensitivity cases, and specific diligence or renegotiation conditions. Do not issue an unconditional buy/pass conclusion from incomplete inputs.

## Before delivery

Reconcile loan balances, total costs, sale proceeds, equity contributions, and profit. Check rate units and draw timing; exclude unspent reserves from expense while disclosing liquidity needs; keep timelines conditional.
